How Negative Net Cost Works
Net Cost Formula:

Net Cost = Total 5-Year Costs - Residual Value


For Kia Niro PHEV (2019):

Purchase Price: €22,000
Total 5-Year Costs: €16,315.83 (depreciation + fuel + electricity + insurance + maintenance + tax)
Residual Value after 5 years: €17,013.75
Net Cost: €16,315.83 - €17,013.75 = -€697.92
Why This Happens
1. Low Depreciation on Used Cars
The Kia Niro PHEV (2019) is already 6 years old when you buy it (in 2025). Older cars depreciate much slower:

Year 1: 7% depreciation
Year 2: 6%
Year 3: 5%
Year 4: 4%
Year 5: 3%
Total depreciation over 5 years: Only ~€5,000 (from €22,000 to €17,014)

2. Low Running Costs
PHEVs have relatively low operating costs:

Fuel: €784/year (only 30% of driving on gasoline)
Electricity: €205/year (70% electric driving)
Maintenance: €880-1,040/year (moderate)
Insurance: €250-300/year (low value = low insurance)
Tax: €97/year
3. The Math Breakdown
Purchase Price:           €22,000
After 5 years worth:      €17,014  (depreciation: €4,986)

5-Year Operating Costs:
- Depreciation:           €4,986
- Fuel (5 years):         €3,920
- Electricity (5 years):  €1,025
- Insurance (5 years):    €1,410
- Maintenance (5 years):  €4,800
- Tax (5 years):          €485
TOTAL COSTS:              €16,316

Residual Value:           €17,014

Net Cost: €16,316 - €17,014 = -€698


What Does This Mean?
You could theoretically:

Buy the car for €22,000
Drive it for 5 years
Spend €11,330 on running costs (fuel, electricity, insurance, maintenance, tax)
Sell it for €17,014
Total out of pocket: €22,000 + €11,330 - €17,014 = €16,316
But if you think of it differently:

The car "costs" you €16,316 total over 5 years
But it's still worth €17,014
So your "net cost" is negative
Real-World Interpretation
In practice, this means:

Very low cost of ownership - you're essentially driving for just the operating costs
Strong residual value - the car holds its value well
Good investment - older PHEVs in good condition are in demand
Market conditions - assumes stable used car market

Important Caveats
⚠️ This assumes:

You can actually sell the car for the calculated residual value
No major repairs or accidents
Normal wear and tear
Stable used car market
The depreciation model is accurate
In reality, you're still spending money to own and operate the vehicle - the negative net cost just shows that the vehicle retains enough value that your total ownership cost is very low compared to the purchase price.
